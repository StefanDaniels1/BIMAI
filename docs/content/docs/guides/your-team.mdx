## Roles

bimai has a catalogue of roles. Your team is a small selection from it, never all of them.

| Role | What it does for you |
|---|---|
| **Coordinator** | Runs the team, follows the project's workflows, talks to you. Always on the team. |
| **Requirements & Risk Manager** | Requirements, verification status, risks and mitigations. Needs a Relatics export or similar. |
| **Planning Analyst** | Milestones, critical path, slipping deliverables. Needs a Primavera or MS Project planning. |
| **Information Manager** | BEP and EIR compliance, naming, status codes, delivery readiness. |
| **Model Checker** | Queries models and checks them against the agreements. |
| **Issue Manager** | Triages, clusters and follows up issues. |
| **Scribe** | Minutes, reports and the decision log. |
| **Mentor** | Explains VS Code and what bimai does, if you're new to it. |
| **Builder** | Adds new tools to bimai. Only for people who build. |

## How your team is chosen

A role only joins if it serves something you asked help with **and** the data it needs exists.
No Relatics export and no interest in requirements means no Requirements & Risk Manager. A team has at
most five members. Your team file records why each member is there:

| Member | Why |
|---|---|
| Coordinator | Always |
| Requirements & Risk Manager | Goals: requirements, risks · Data: Relatics export |
| Planning Analyst | Goal: planning · Data: Primavera XML |
| Scribe | Goals: meetings, design progress reporting |

## Changing your team

- `bimai team review` suggests removing members you haven't used for four weeks, and adding one when
  new data or goals appear. Nothing changes without your confirmation.
- `bimai onboard --update` re-runs the interview when your role or the project phase changes.
- Removing a member keeps what it learned, so adding it back later restores it.

## See your team

```bash
bimai graph team
```

draws your team, their models and the tools each may use, as a diagram in VS Code.
